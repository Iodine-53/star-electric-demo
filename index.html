1 · IS IT THE PANEL OR JUST A BAD BREAKER?

5 signs your house is asking for an electrician

Your breaker trips twice a month.

A breaker that trips once in a blue moon is doing its job. One that trips every few weeks is telling you the circuit is overloaded or the breaker is worn out. We find the real cause in one visit — most fixes run $149 to $320. Wait too long and a $200 fix turns into a panel conversation.

Sounds like you? Get a free look →

Outlets feel warm to the touch.

Outlets should never feel warm. Warmth means a loose connection or an overloaded wire, and loose connections are how electrical fires start. This one can't wait. Call us and we make it safe the same day — usually under $200.

Warm outlet? Call now →

Lights dim when the microwave runs.

When a big appliance kicks on and the lights dip, your panel is straining to keep up. It's the classic sign of a 100-amp panel feeding a 200-amp house. A load check takes twenty minutes and tells you exactly where you stand.

Run the load check below →

You smell something hot near the panel.

A hot or fishy smell near your breaker panel means plastic is overheating somewhere inside. This is the one symptom you never sleep on — turn off what you can and call an electrician now. Emergency call-outs start at $199.

Smell something? Call now →

Your panel is 25+ years old — or says Federal Pacific.

Old panels weren't built for today's loads, and some brands — Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco — are ones Florida insurers flag by name. If your panel is older than your car, get a free look. Most swaps take half a day and power is back by evening.

Why your 100A panel is the bottleneck

Most Clearwater homes built before 1990 came with a 100-amp panel. Back then it was plenty — one TV, no computer, no EV in the driveway.

Today that same panel feeds central AC, a dryer, a microwave, and maybe a charger. The math doesn't fit anymore, so the breaker trips — and every reset wears it a little thinner.

A 200-amp service change gives your house the headroom it was designed for. We pull the Clearwater permit, the power is off for one workday, and every breaker gets labeled before we leave.
